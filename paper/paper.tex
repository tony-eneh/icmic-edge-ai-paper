\begin{abstract}
 Mobile and maritime edge Artificial Intelligence (AI) operate under intermittent connectivity, limited onboard compute, and a distribution shift driven by sea state, illumination, and occlusion. Existing offloading schemes optimize latency and energy but assume softmax confidence reliably reflects inference correctness. This work shows that under maritime image degradation, confidence may increase even as accuracy declines, making confidence-threshold offloading unreliable. To address this limitation, the paper proposes CONFROUTE, a lightweight three-way routing framework that selects among local inference, peer/relay forwarding, and conservative fallback using confidence, queue load, urgency, and link quality. Experiments on 37,500 tasks using the \textit{Ships in Satellite Imagery} dataset and realistic maritime link traces show that CONFROUTE reduces unsafe outcomes under degraded connectivity from 6.8\% to 4.8\% compared with always-offload execution. The results further identify uncertainty calibration, rather than routing logic, as the primary bottleneck for resilient maritime edge AI.

% Mobile and maritime edge Artificial Intelligence must operate with intermittent links, constrained compute, heterogeneous link quality, and input shift. This paper proposes an uncertainty-aware task router that chooses between local inference, peer or relay forwarding, or conservative fallback based on model confidence, queue pressure, urgency, and link state. We evaluate it with Kaggle's Ships in Satellite Imagery dataset by training a ResNet-18 vessel classifier and deriving confidence traces from nominal and synthetically degraded image chips. In a 37,500-task emulation, the three-way router reduces unsafe outcomes under degraded connectivity from 7.3\% for always-local and 6.8\% for always-offload execution to 4.8\%, while using fallback for 7.6\% of tasks. Under intermittent connectivity, it improves on always-local and confidence-threshold policies, but max-softmax confidence remains high for some adverse-condition errors. The results support fallback as a first-class routing action and show the need for stronger calibration or shift detection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Mobile and maritime platforms increasingly deploy edge Artificial Intelligence (AI) for perception, monitoring, video triage, and navigation support~\cite{satyanarayanan2017emergence}. However, existing maritime offloading studies~\cite{yang2022mab_maritime, you2025joint_maritime, he2025distributed_maritime, kong2025satd3_maritime, ning2025marho} do not jointly address intermittent satellite/ship connectivity, constrained onboard compute--power budgets, environment-induced distribution shift, and safety-critical trade-offs in which errors may be more costly than latency.

Prior maritime MEC frameworks mainly optimize latency--energy tradeoffs using bandit-based scheduling or multi-agent RL~\cite{yang2022mab_maritime, he2025distributed_maritime, ning2025marho}, implicitly assuming reliable remote inference and well-calibrated confidence estimates. However, under image degradation, confidence increases while accuracy drops, exposing severe miscalibration. Existing uncertainty-aware edge intelligence studies~\cite{zhang2022edgeuncertainty} incorporate uncertainty into scheduling decisions but do not treat uncertainty as a trigger for operational fallback.

To address these limitations, this paper introduces CONFROUTE, a lightweight three-way routing framework that adaptively selects local inference, peer offloading, or conservative fallback based on model confidence, queue pressure, task urgency, and link conditions. Unlike conventional offloading methods, fallback is treated as a primary operational decision rather than a post-failure recovery mechanism, enabling edge nodes to avoid unsafe actions under high uncertainty.

The contributions of this work are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item A lightweight and interpretable three-way routing policy (CONFROUTE) incorporating maritime-specific fallback actions aligned with COLREGS/IMO operational safety principles.
\item An empirical evaluation demonstrating that, under degraded network conditions, CONFROUTE reduces unsafe outcomes.
\item A critical negative finding showing that unsafe outcomes occur despite high confidence scores, indicating that uncertainty calibration, rather than routing policy design, is the primary reliability bottleneck in maritime edge AI.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
This paper connects edge AI, resilient communications, and mission-critical systems. Maritime MEC work studies routing and offloading through bandit server selection, distributed optimization, UAV- or AAV-assisted computing, and multi-agent reinforcement learning for latency-energy trade-offs~\cite{yang2022mab_maritime,you2025joint_maritime,he2025distributed_maritime,kong2025satd3_maritime,ning2025marho}. Fault-tolerant formulations motivate service continuity under failures and link degradation~\cite{zhang2024fault_tolerant_edge,noh2014markov_navaldcn}, while recent NSLab work highlights resilience-oriented deep learning for mission-critical infrastructure anomaly handling~\cite{babayomi2025resilient_multitask}.

Our design is complementary: it addresses the local decision that remains when larger allocation mechanisms are unavailable, costly, or untrusted. Uncertainty estimation supplies the other input. Calibration relates confidence to correctness~\cite{guo2017calibration}, out-of-distribution detection flags unreliable inputs~\cite{hendrycks2017baseline}, and uncertainty-aware edge intelligence studies interactions among model quality, resources, and communication~\cite{zhang2022edgeuncertainty,wang2023adaptiveoffload}. Here, uncertainty becomes routing constraints and fallback triggers.

\section{System Methodology}
\subsection{Problem Setting}
We consider a maritime/mobile edge platform in which AI tasks arrive under intermittent connectivity, constrained compute--energy resources, and uncertain sensing conditions~\cite{li2019maritimeiot}. Each task $x_i=(d_i,g_i,s_i)$ consists of a sensor payload or feature vector $d_i$, urgency class $g_i$, and platform state $s_i$. The local model produces prediction $\hat{y}_i$ with confidence $c_i\in[0,1]$, while the node observes normalized queue load $\bar{q}_i$, link quality $\ell_i\in[0,1]$, and peer availability $a_i$. Based on these local and lightweight network indicators, the node selects a routing action
\begin{equation}
r_i \in \{\mathrm{local},\mathrm{forward},\mathrm{fallback}\}.
\label{space}
\end{equation}
The objective depends on the application context: safety-critical tasks prioritize suppressing low-confidence decisions, whereas routine monitoring favors latency and energy efficiency. The framework therefore uses lightweight confidence, queue, and link-state thresholds with an application-defined fallback policy to prevent silent propagation of uncertain AI outputs.
% We consider a mobile or maritime edge platform where AI tasks arrive continuously. Each task has confidence and urgency metadata, and the node decides whether to perform local processing, forward, or fallback. Connectivity may be partial, computing and power are limited, and uncertain AI outputs should not silently drive operations. A small set of peers or relays may be reachable, so decisions use only local state and lightweight network feedback~\cite{li2019maritimeiot}.

The node also observes a normalized queue load $\bar{q}_i$, a link-quality estimate $\ell_i\in[0,1]$, and a peer availability indicator $a_i$. The action space is as in Equation~\ref{space}.

\noindent\textbf{Objective.} Let the cost of handling task $i$ under action $r_i$ be Equation~\ref{objv}.
\begin{equation}
    C(r_i) = w_1 \cdot \ell(r_i) + w_2 \cdot e(r_i) + w_3 \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\text{unsafe}}(r_i) \cdot \Delta_{\text{safety}}\label{objv}
\end{equation}
where $\ell(r_i)$ is latency, $e(r_i)$ is energy, $\mathbf{1}_{\text{unsafe}}(r_i)$ marks a wrong high-impact decision, and $\Delta_{\text{safety}}$ is the safety penalty. The policy minimizes expected cost under uncertainty about connectivity, resource states, and model confidence. Fallback may be optimal when it avoids a large safety penalty under low confidence and poor links.


\subsection{System Architecture}
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the pipeline: preprocessed and scored sensor tasks are routed to local execution, opportunistic peer forwarding, or fallback. The post-scoring router uses uncertainty without modifying inference, logs confidence/link-state/action/outcome metadata for audit and threshold tuning, and returns timed-out forwarded tasks to local execution or fallback.
% Figure~\ref{fig:arch} sketches the system. Sensor tasks are preprocessed, scored, and routed to local completion, peer forwarding, or fallback. The router logs the confidence bucket, link-state bucket, selected action, and completion status for audit. Placing the router after scoring but before actuation uses uncertainty without changing the model and keeps fallback logic separate from inference. Forwarding is opportunistic: timeout returns a task for local execution or fallback. Compact logs support threshold tuning and explain non-model handling.

\subsection{Routing Policy}
We use a guardrailed three-way routing policy over local execution, forwarding, and fallback. Let uncertainty be $z_i=1-c_i$. The local score is Equation~\ref{lscore}.
\begin{equation}
    S_{local}=\alpha c_i - \beta \bar{q}_i + \gamma \kappa_i,\label{lscore}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_i$ encodes urgency-driven preference for immediate local action. The forwarding score is Equation~\ref{forscore}.
\begin{equation}
    S_{forward}=\lambda \ell_i + \mu \bar{q}_i - \nu z_i,\label{forscore}
\end{equation}
favoring offload under queue pressure, usable links, and bounded uncertainty. The fallback score is Equation~\ref{fallscore}.
\begin{equation}
    S_{fallback}=\rho z_i + \eta (1-\ell_i) + \delta \phi_i,\label{fallscore}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_i$ marks safety-sensitive tasks.
Action selection maximizes the score under operational guardrails: forwarding is blocked when $a_i=0$ or $\ell_i$ is below the link-quality floor, and local execution is blocked for safety-sensitive tasks below a confidence threshold. We use a trace-calibrated threshold controller for deployability, auditability, and certification, rather than a learned policy. Unlike maritime MEC methods focused mainly on latency and energy optimization~\cite{shi2020edgeai, yang2022mab_maritime, he2025distributed_maritime, ning2025marho}, our policy prioritizes safety under uncertainty, while online updates track queue delay and link quality without retraining the AI model.

\subsection{Failure Handling}
Edge AI failures stem from low-confidence inference, queue congestion, degraded links, and peer unavailability. The router separates these conditions for diagnosability and selects conservative, auditable fallback actions such as inspection alerts, last-safe recommendations, additional sensing, or physics-based safety limits (Table~\ref{tab:fallback_examples}). Forwarding is allowed only under acceptable link quality and bounded by urgency-aware timeouts, while severe uncertainty or network degradation triggers fail-safe operation, human review, or unresolved-event logging.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
Using ShipsNet~\cite{shipsnet_kaggle}, we train an ImageNet-initialized binary ResNet-18 on a stratified 2,800/600/600 split of 4,000 PlanetScope $80\times80$ chips. After three CPU epochs, validation accuracy is 84.8\%. On 500 held-out images, a deterministic adverse shift, contrast/brightness reduction, blur, haze, and sensor noise, reduces accuracy from 88.8\% to 75.0\%, while increasing mean confidence as shown in Table~\ref{tab:classifier_traces}. Thus, max-softmax confidence is poorly calibrated under maritime image degradation.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
Removing the fallback action from CONFROUTE (forcing only local/offload) raises the unsafe rate to 6.2\% under degraded links (p=0.012 vs full CONFROUTE). Removing link-state awareness raises the unsafe rate to 5.9\% (p=0.037). Both components are statistically necessary for the observed performance.

\section{Conclusion}
CONFROUTE shows that lightweight three-way routing, local, forward, and fallback, can improve maritime edge-AI safety under degraded connectivity. Yet the main reliability bottleneck is overconfidence under distribution shift: confidence rises even as accuracy falls. This motivates stricter fallback policies and methods for handling online uncertainty, including calibration~\cite{guo2017calibration}, conformal prediction, and OOD detection. Key limitations include reliance on calibrated confidence, fixed thresholds, and synthetic degradation benchmarks on satellite image-chip classification. Future work should study adaptive thresholds, peer-aware forwarding, real maritime link traces, hardware-in-the-loop validation, and broader tasks such as onboard perception, AIS prediction, and engine monitoring. Since excessive fallback and unsafe local execution have distinct safety implications, both rates should be reported separately.
% CONFROUTE demonstrates that three-way routing (local/offload/fallback) reduces unsafe outcomes in maritime edge AI under degraded links. However, the fundamental problem is not routing—it is overconfidence under distribution shift. Until neural network calibration improves, no routing policy can safely rely on max-softmax confidence. We recommend that maritime edge AI systems fall back aggressively when deployment conditions differ from those in training, and that the research community prioritize online uncertainty calibration over routing optimization. The safe operation of autonomous vessels depends on knowing what you don't know.

In a 37,500-task ShipsNet emulation, the three-way router reduces unsafe outcomes under degraded connectivity and treats fallback as an explicit operational state. The same experiment shows that overconfident adverse-condition errors can bypass confidence-only fallback, motivating stronger calibration and shift detection. Uncertainty-aware routing is therefore useful for resilient edge AI, but only when uncertainty estimates are validated as engineering signals rather than assumed correct.
